# Research method — from internet source to defensible evidence

## 1. Discover
Use official agencies, standards bodies, declassified archives, university courseware, public research papers, and community reference projects. Search in original languages when possible and document translation methods.

## 2. Identify the exact kind of evidence
- **Physical or mathematical principle:** evaluate against primary scientific and standards sources.
- **Institutional self-description:** establish only what the institution publicly claims.
- **Third-party government assessment:** name the assessing government and the assessment date.
- **Historical primary record:** distinguish what was recorded at the time from modern interpretation.
- **Expert commentary:** independent but potentially contestable; record credentials and uncertainty.
- **Inference:** explicitly state the assumptions, alternatives and disconfirming observations.

## 3. Triangulate without manufacturing certainty
A document about an organizational mandate, a press report, and a manufacturer brochure may corroborate a broad concept; they do **not** collectively reveal an undisclosed live capability. Record corroboration only when sources make independently testable overlapping claims. Secondary reports repeating the same original are a single evidence lineage.

## 4. Structure the claim
`claim → sources → evidence type → geographic/time scope → uncertainty → review history`. Use exact URL and dated title; add author, publication date and page number when reviewed.

## 5. Mark unknowns
Use three independent dimensions: (a) source reliability, (b) corroboration, (c) completeness/recency. A confident source statement can coexist with large uncertainty about operational reality.

## 6. Research gaps ethically
Examples: missing official mandates, lack of translated archival histories, differences in published doctrine, or unexplained terminology. Do not attempt to fill gaps with live observation of protected communications or tactical targeting inference.

## 7. Snapshot restrictions
Keep metadata cards for all lawful links. Full webpage/PDF/image copies require verified permission or license, acceptable site terms, access rights and a clear use case. Store hashes, original URL, capture date and exact rights decision. See `SOURCE_POLICY.md`.

## 8. Maintain the atlas
Monthly: review broken links and official publications. Quarterly: validate major claims, update country profiles and build provenance coverage metrics. Annually: audit rights and course content. These are suggested editorial cadences; **no automatic monitoring is currently running**.

## Edition 0.4 repeatability rule

Newly discovered source cards are not independent empirical confirmation; new research notes are candidates until reviewed; measured in-sample packet-size reduction is not model compute reduction. Every release must expose its experimental protocols and data-quality fixes.
