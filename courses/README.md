# SIGNAL ATLAS ACADEMY — 30-lesson open course

**Audience:** curious humans, students, RF researchers, historians, policy analysts, and AI agents. Prerequisites: none; basic algebra helps. Activities use synthetic, public, or explicitly authorized data.

| Lesson | Topic | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01–10 | Signal foundations, spectrum, receiver chain, SIGINT distinctions, doctrine, history, and evidence graph | Explain core concepts and build a bounded attributed claim. |
| 11–13 | Sampling, archives, and agent provenance labs | Reproduce a signal example and preserve evidence lineage. |
| 14–17 | Wave physics, Fourier methods, paper reading, and hypothesis design | Separate established science, model assumptions, results, and candidate hypotheses. |
| 18–20 | Synthetic signal experiment, evidence packets, and cross-domain inference | Test controlled examples and keep inference separate from evidence. |
| 21–24 | NOAA solar-radio-flux data, SAR products, calibration assumptions, and packet integrity | Work with dated observations and explain the limits of retrieved metadata. |
| 25 | [Frequency stability and measurement limits](25-frequency-stability-and-measurement.md) | Reproduce a synthetic Allan-deviation calculation; distinguish stability, accuracy, and uncertainty. |
| 26 | [Public records and source criticism](26-public-records-and-source-criticism.md) | Trace a public historical claim through item, archive, institutional account, or interpretation. |
| 27 | [Join space-weather observations](27_space_weather_data_join.md) | Build and interpret a date-keyed F10.7 and estimated planetary Kp comparison. |
| 28 | [Align solar-wind time series](28_solar_wind_time_alignment.md) | Pair minute-scale plasma and magnetic-field records by timestamp and source. |
| 29 | [Navigate public SIGINT history](29-public-sigint-history.md) | Distinguish catalogs, recollections, institutional histories, and interpretations. |
| 30 | [Compile observations with provenance](30-compile-observations-and-provenance.md) | Trace raw, derived, and synthetic datasets through provenance and search. |

## Pedagogy and assessment

Each lesson asks learners to read, define, observe, apply, verify, and explain a limitation. Grade the capstone on source quality (25%), scientific distinctions (20%), precise attribution (20%), uncertainty (20%), and navigation (15%). Use the [capstone assignment](CAPSTONE.md).

For every claim, cite its original URL and distinguish source testimony from independent verification. Open the source yourself; an Atlas card is not the complete source. Candidate research notes remain provisional. Exercises do not ask students to intercept live private transmissions, reverse engineer current military systems, or publish sensitive operational details.
