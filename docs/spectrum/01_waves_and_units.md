# Waves, frequency and wavelength

Frequency **f** is cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz). Wavelength **λ** is the distance between corresponding points on a wave. In vacuum, an electromagnetic wave travels at approximately **c = 299,792,458 m/s**, so **λ = c / f** and **f = c / λ**. In materials, wave speed depends on medium.

Illustrative calculations (approximate in vacuum):

| Frequency | Wavelength | Learning interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 kHz | 299.8 km | A general low-frequency mathematical example |
| 1 MHz | 299.8 m | A general radio-frequency example |
| 100 MHz | 3.00 m | VHF example; not a specific channel |
| 1 GHz | 0.300 m | UHF example |
| 10 GHz | 3.00 cm | SHF example |

Other quantities: amplitude (signal strength), phase, bandwidth (frequency span), noise floor, sample rate, and power. A frequency alone cannot reveal message contents, identity, intent, or legality.
