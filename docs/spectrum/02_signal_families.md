# Signals, information and modulation

A **carrier** is a physical oscillation used to convey information. Modulation varies a carrier parameter. Common families include amplitude modulation (AM), frequency modulation (FM), phase modulation (PM), digital phase-shift keying (PSK), frequency-shift keying (FSK) and orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM).

In a **spectrogram**, horizontal position is time, vertical position is frequency, and color/brightness represents relative energy. This describes a signal's distribution, not necessarily its meaning. A spectrogram cannot by itself prove a waveform's ownership or military role.

**Digitization basics:** sample rate defines how often a signal is measured. Digital IQ samples encode in-phase and quadrature components; SigMF provides metadata for recordings. Use synthetic examples or authorized datasets.

**Practical exercise:** generate two mathematical tones in a spreadsheet (e.g. sin(2π·100t) and sin(2π·200t)), compare their periods, and infer their frequencies. No receiver or transmitted signal is needed. References: SRC-005, SRC-006.
