# United States — public SIGINT research profile

**Published institution / framework:** NSA/CSS with military cryptologic integration  
**Seed evidence:** Direct primary-source mission statements; military integration and historical archives.

## Original sources
- **SRC-009** [Signals Intelligence Overview](https://www.nsa.gov/Signals-Intelligence/Overview/) — US government explanation of foreign SIGINT, missions, stakeholders, and limitations.
- **SRC-010** [NSA Signals Intelligence FAQs](https://www.nsa.gov/about/faqs/sigint-faqs/) — Public discussion of US SIGINT authorities, oversight, and compliance.
- **SRC-025** [Cryptologic Documents Collection](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/bibliographies-and-research-guides/research-guides/cryptologic-documents.html) — Catalog of US Navy historical cryptologic documents, heavily focused on WWI and WWII.
- **SRC-027** [Office of Naval Intelligence Heritage](https://www.oni.navy.mil/About-Us/Heritage/) — Public naval intelligence history including historical SIGINT support.
- **SRC-028** [How the Intelligence Community Works](https://www.intel.gov/how-the-ic-works) — Public intelligence cycle overview and distinction between collection disciplines.

## Established scope
The above items are public **research entrypoints**. They can document the nature of published roles, institutional context, and historical or doctrinal descriptions, subject to source dates and limitations.

## Uncertainty / attribution
Published self-description may not be an independent verification of operational effectiveness.

## Open scholarly questions
- Which statutes, parliamentary/public oversight reports or independent histories deepen the published picture?
- How does the available doctrine define collection, analysis, defense support and legal limitations?
- Which materials are historical, outdated, retracted, replaced or incomplete?

**Boundary:** no cataloging of current unit positions, live emitter frequencies, private intercepted traffic or tactical targeting data.
