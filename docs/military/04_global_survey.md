# Comparative worldwide SIGINT survey

The following table describes **public research entrypoints**, not a military capability ranking. Profiles with only third-party assessments must remain attributed.

| Country / alliance | Public research entrypoint | Evidence character |
|---|---|---|
| United States | NSA/CSS; Navy histories | US agency self-description + historical archives |
| United Kingdom | GCHQ; MoD ISR doctrine | Government mission and doctrine statements |
| Canada | CSE | Government mandate and legal sources |
| Australia | ASD | Government mission and definitions |
| New Zealand | GCSB | Government mission and legal source |
| Sweden | FRA | Government mission overview |
| France | DGSE and military DRM | Government descriptions including fusion framework |
| Germany | BND brochure | Government source; link access recheck needed |
| Israel | IDF Military Intelligence | Historical official web description; verify current roles |
| Japan | Defense Intelligence Headquarters; defense white paper | Japanese government primary sources |
| Netherlands | MIVD; statutory framework | Dutch government primary sources |
| Norway | Norwegian Intelligence Service | Government mission (SIGINT-specific scope still to research) |
| China | 2025 US DoD annual report | **External US assessment only** |
| Russia | US CRS report | **External US assessment only** |
| NATO | EMW and JISR public doctrine | Alliance doctrinal framework, not a state |

Follow the original source IDs in `data/entities.jsonl`. Some countries with limited accessible official information are intentionally absent from the seeded set. Missing coverage is **a research gap, not an absence of SIGINT capability**.

Comparisons should focus on published functions, governance, educational terminology, historical evolution, and changes in doctrine. Do not extrapolate tactical signal parameters or hidden organizational details.
